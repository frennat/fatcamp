# Reaching out to lifting creators

Companion to [MARKETING.md](MARKETING.md). Read the first section before you
send anything — it changes who you should be messaging.

---

## Why I did not just hand you a list of big accounts

Two reasons, one practical and one strategic.

**Practical:** Instagram is login-walled, and the "top powerlifting influencers"
pages that rank on Google are SEO aggregators with no timestamps. I pulled one
and it lists follower counts with no indication of when they were measured.
Handing you forty handles off that is how you spend three weeks DMing accounts
that are dormant, have pivoted, or were never the size the page claimed. You
have Instagram access and I do not — you can build a current list in an hour
with the method below, and it will be worth more than anything I could recite.

**Strategic, and this is the bigger one:** **you have nothing to ask a creator
to do yet.**

A creator's entire value is "download this." Right now the answer to "where?"
is TestFlight, which needs an Apple ID invite or a public link you do not have.
The web app works, but "go to a GitHub URL in Safari" is not a call to action
anyone with an audience will put their name behind.

So a 200k-follower lifter who somehow said yes today would send traffic to a
thing their followers cannot straightforwardly install. That is the worst
possible first impression, and you only get one with each of them.

**The move: get on the App Store first, then do this properly.** Meanwhile
there is a tier that works right now, and it is not influencers.

## The tier that will actually answer you

Not "influencers." **Local coaches and gym owners, roughly 1k–20k followers.**

Why they respond when bigger accounts do not:

- They have no rate card. Anyone above ~50k does, and it starts in the hundreds
  per post and climbs fast. You have no budget, so that tier is a dead end
  until you do.
- Their audience is people they know by name. A recommendation from them
  carries far more weight per follower than a big account's does.
- They have a real problem you solve: writing programmes for clients takes them
  hours a week, and clients who train alone go off-plan between sessions.
- **They are local**, which matters more than it sounds — your posters are
  concentrated around East Aurora and Buffalo, and your leaderboards only feel
  alive with regional density. Twenty lifters in one town beats two hundred
  scattered.

The unlock with this tier is not "market my app." It is **"try this and tell me
what is wrong with it."** People who would ignore a promo request will often
answer a genuine request for criticism — and a coach who gave you feedback and
saw you act on it becomes an advocate without ever being asked.

## Build the list yourself, in about an hour

1. **Instagram search → Places → Buffalo, NY** (then Orchard Park, Hamburg,
   East Aurora, Amherst). Scroll recent posts. Anyone posting their own lifting
   regularly is a candidate.
2. **Hashtags, local first:** `#buffalofitness`, `#wnyfitness`, `#716fitness`,
   `#buffalogym`. Then broader: `#powerbuilding`, `#homegymlife`,
   `#garagegym` — home-gym people are your sharpest fit, because working around
   limited equipment is exactly what your app does.
3. **Follow the gyms, not just the people.** Find independent gyms and CrossFit
   boxes in WNY, then look at who they tag and who comments. That is a
   pre-filtered list of locally-connected lifters.
4. **Check the follower-to-comment ratio before you add anyone.** An account
   with 30k followers and four comments a post has bought them. One with 3k
   followers and forty real comments is worth ten of those.

Aim for **thirty names**. Ten "coach or gym owner," ten "serious local lifter,"
ten "home-gym content." Put them in a sheet with handle, rough follower count,
and one specific thing about them — you need that last column for the DM.

Send me the list when you have it and I will help you rank it and spot the ones
not worth the message.

## The message

Response rate is decided almost entirely by whether the first line proves you
actually looked at their account. Everything else is secondary.

**For a coach or gym owner:**

> Hey [name] — saw your [specific thing: the deload video, the garage setup
> post, the thing about programming around a bad shoulder]. I built a workout
> generator that writes a session around whatever equipment someone has and
> whatever they can't load right now — injuries, limitations, three dumbbells,
> whatever. I'm in Buffalo, it's free, and I'm trying to find out where it
> falls over before I put it on the App Store. Would you try it and tell me
> what's wrong with it? [link] — no signup, works in the browser.

**For a lifter with an audience:**

> Hey [name] — [specific thing]. I made a thing that spits out a session in
> about a minute based on your gear and how beat up you are. No account, no
> email. I'm looking for people who train seriously to tell me where it's
> stupid. [link] if you want a look.

Rules that matter more than the wording:

- **Ask for criticism, not promotion.** "Would you post about this" is a
  transaction they have no reason to accept. "Tell me what's wrong with it" is
  a favour that costs them two minutes and flatters their expertise.
- **One specific detail from their account in the first sentence**, or do not
  send it. This is the whole thing.
- **Never send the same message twice in a row.** If it is not landing after
  fifteen, the message is wrong — change it, do not send thirty more.
- **No follow-up if they ignore you.** A second DM converts almost nobody and
  costs you the option of a warm approach later, once you have users.
- **Send 5–10 a day, not 40.** Instagram rate-limits DMs to non-followers and
  will restrict the account.

## When the bigger accounts become worth it

After you have App Store presence and a few hundred real users. At that point
you have the two things they need — an install link that works, and evidence
other people use it — and the conversation is completely different.

Expect to pay. Mid-tier lifting accounts (50k–200k) run a few hundred to a few
thousand per post depending on engagement. Widely known powerlifting names sit
far above that. Do not approach that tier with an ask for free promotion; it
reads as not understanding what they do, and it closes the door.

**One thing worth knowing:** a paid post from a big account converts worse than
people expect for utility apps. Fitness audiences follow those accounts for the
person, not for tool recommendations. Thirty local coaches who genuinely use
the app will out-perform one 200k post, and cost nothing.
